The case for slow software

Why the tools we love most are the ones that refuse to hurry us, and what a decade of “instant” has cost.

By Inés Vidal · October 24, 2026 · 14 min read
Sunset over the Tagus. Photograph by Rui Costa.

The first piece of software I truly loved took eleven seconds to open. I know because I timed it, the way you time a kettle: not out of impatience, but because the waiting had become part of the ritual. In those eleven seconds I decided what I was going to write.

Somewhere along the way we decided that every pause was a defect. Spinners became skeletons, skeletons became optimistic updates, and the tools got faster than the people using them.
