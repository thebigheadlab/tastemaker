The quiet case for slower interfaces

Frank Alder · Oct 18, 2026 · Highlighted 4

We spent a decade shaving milliseconds off every interaction, and it worked: software feels instant now. But somewhere along the way, instant stopped feeling like a courtesy and started feeling like pressure. A list that refreshes before you've finished reading it is not fast. It is impatient.

Consider the humble loading state. Designers treat it as a failure to hide, yet the best ones do something else entirely: they give the person a beat to decide whether they still want what they asked for. Reading apps understand this. A page turn that takes a quarter of a second is not latency, it is punctuation.

None of this is an argument for slow software. It is an argument for interfaces that move at the speed of attention rather than the speed of the network. Those are different speeds, and most of the time the first one is lower.

What would it look like to design for that? Fewer things animating at once. Transitions that finish before the next one begins. Notifications that wait for a pause instead of interrupting a sentence. In short, an interface with manners.
